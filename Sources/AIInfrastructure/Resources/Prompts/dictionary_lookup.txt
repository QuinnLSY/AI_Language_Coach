Rules:
- "|" separates the fields INSIDE one line. It must NEVER appear inside a field. Use "、" or "，" to separate items within a field.
- The key name is only a key. Never write a key name as the value, and never leave a value empty.
- OMIT a key entirely when you have nothing real to say for it. Never write "-" as a value.
- word: exactly the word that was asked, unchanged.
- phonetic_us / phonetic_uk: write BOTH for a single word, and give your best real transcription — never leave the slashes empty. If the headword is a phrase or a sentence, omit both lines.
- sense: one line per part of speech, most common first. List EVERY common sense of that part of speech, separated by "；". 2-4 lines at most. Do not collapse a multi-sense word into one vague gloss.
- collocation: the fixed expressions a learner actually meets with this word (2-5 lines). Each one needs a real example sentence and its {native_language} translation. Omit the key entirely if the word has no common fixed expression.
- example: 2-3 natural sentences in {learning_language}, each with its {native_language} translation. Do not repeat a collocation example.
- morphology: only when the word is clearly built from parts (prefix / root / suffix). Write the parts joined by " + ", each as "part = meaning", then "=>" and the combined {native_language} meaning. Omit the line when the word is not decomposable.
- etymology: one or two {native_language} sentences. Omit the line when you do not know it — do not invent one.
- derivative: real words built from the same root (2-4 lines). synonym / antonym: 2-4 real words each. Every one needs its part of speech AND its {native_language} meaning.
- family_root / family_branch / family_member describe the WORD FAMILY TREE — a group of words
  sharing one root, so the learner can memorise them together. Write these ONLY when the headword
  really belongs to such a family (a shared Latin/Greek root that several words are built on).
  When it does not, omit all three keys — a family of one is not a family.
  * family_root: the root itself, e.g. `riv`, plus its {native_language} meaning, e.g. `河`.
  * family_branch: 2-4 words that are the main branches built on that root. The fourth field
    shows how the word is built from the root, e.g. `riv 河：溪流 + -al 与……有关的`.
  * family_member: 1-3 words hanging under ONE branch word. The FIRST field must be that branch
    word, copied exactly as you wrote it in its family_branch line. The fifth field shows how it
    is built, e.g. `rival 竞争者：对手 + -ry 名词后缀：状态/集合`.
  * One branch word may itself be the headword, and the headword usually appears somewhere in the
    tree — write it as a branch or a member exactly where it belongs, never in a place of its own.
  * Every branch and member needs its own part of speech and {native_language} meaning.
- INFLECTED FORMS. {base_form_hint}
  * If the headword is an inflected form — a plural, a third-person -s form, an -ing form, an
    -ed form, a comparative or superlative, or an irregular form (went, children, better…) —
    then it is NOT a word of its own. Do the opposite of "copy the headword":
    write the entry for its BASE FORM (phonetics, senses, examples all for the base form),
    set "word:" to that base form, and add ONE extra line in the form shown above.
  * Example: asked for `subtitles` → `word: subtitle`, all the entry lines for `subtitle`,
    and `form: subtitles = subtitle | 复数形式，指影片的字幕`.
  * Omit the `form:` line when the headword is not an inflected form.
  * NEVER answer with a sentence, a note, or a translation instead of these lines. A reply that
    is not made of "key: value" lines is a broken reply.
- Everything is written in {native_language} (Chinese) unless the field explicitly asks for {learning_language}.
- No numbering, no bullets, no quotes, no markdown, no extra lines, no commentary.
